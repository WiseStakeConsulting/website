First, the concept

Most crypto moves money. DePIN moves the physical world.

DePIN — decentralized physical infrastructure networks — uses a blockchain to coordinate real hardware: storage drives, GPUs, wireless hotspots, bandwidth. Independent operators own the machines. Customers pay to use them. The chain verifies the work actually happened, then pays.

01OPERATORS SUPPLY HARDWARE

Real machines, owned by real people, connected to the network — not one corporation's server farm.

02CUSTOMERS PAY FOR SERVICE

Storage, content delivery, bandwidth, compute. Real demand from real users — Sovren's stack (Parler, Play, Shop, Pay) is the demand side.

03PROOF, THEN PAYMENT

Every request produces a signed cryptographic receipt. The chain scores operators on a rolling 30-day window and settles in SOVR.

Follow the money

Where the 40% actually comes from.

Every SOVR settled on the network is split by hard rule: 50% is burned and recycled (structural pressure on the token's price), 25% goes to the operator pool, and 25% funds the company. That operator pool is real revenue from real usage — creators monetizing on Parler and Play, files stored, content delivered, bandwidth served.

When you deposit USDC into the stablecoin pool, your deposit goes toward purchasing the infrastructure — the node licenses and hardware that make the network run. The network's verified work generates SOVR. The pool's revenue is what pays your rate.

This is the difference between a yield backed by subscriber invoices and server fees — and a yield backed by new deposits. DePIN pays from the first kind.

Straight answers

Questions we get first.

Is the 40% guaranteed?
For the current window, yes: the platform guarantees the 40% APR for the next five months. After that window, the rate depends on network demand and SOVR's price — the projections further up this page are the platform team's, not promises. Sovren's whitepaper is explicit that node rewards are never guaranteed long-term, which is exactly why we tell you where the rate comes from instead of just printing the number. Do your own research; nothing here is financial advice.
Why is this one APR and the World Mobile one APY?
Precision. The Sovren pool pays a flat rate that doesn't compound, so it's published as APR. The World Mobile stablecoin strategy compounds, so it's published as APY. We use the honest label for each — compare rates only when you know which is which.
Where exactly does my deposit go?
Into purchasing the pool's infrastructure: Sovren node licenses (NFT-backed, capped at 10,000) and the hardware they run. The network revenue those machines generate is what funds the pool's rate.
What happens when Tier 01 closes?
Later tiers enter at higher prices with more of the rate already behind them. Per the platform team, later pools are projected at lower APRs, while deposits already in Tier 01 are projected to see the pool's return rise toward 60% APY and beyond as network demand grows. Projections, not promises.
Can I get a node license myself instead?
Yes — $3,000 at Tier 01, with a 12-month transfer lockup and 180-day reward vesting. The pool route exists for people who want the infrastructure exposure in stablecoins without operating a node. A free session can help you weigh the two.
How do withdrawals work?
This is a deposit, not a locked stake — closer to a savings account than a certificate of deposit. A withdrawal is processed as a sell order: the platform sells the corresponding position on the back end (it holds the node licenses, so orders are filled from the node side) and pays you out. Per the platform team, sell orders are processed quickly, but it is an order queue, not an instant ATM — treat the timeline as days, not seconds, and plan accordingly.
