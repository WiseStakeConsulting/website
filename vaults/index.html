Why it works

Every other network is owned by a corporation. This one is owned by its people.

Verizon's towers belong to Verizon. When you pay your bill, the revenue flows one way: up.

World Mobile flips it. The network is built out of AirNodes — telecom hardware owned by ordinary people. Buy one, the team deploys it where connectivity is needed, and when subscribers make calls and use data through it, the owner earns. Real hardware, real usage, real earnings in USD.

The vault is the same story one step removed: the network's subscriber revenue funds the pool that pays your 20% APY on stablecoin deposits.

Engine two · 40% APR

The node economy. The math compounds.

Sovren is real-world infrastructure that pays its operators for verifiable work. Deposit USDC, it purchases the node infrastructure, and the network's revenue funds a 40% APR pool — while Tier 01 pricing lasts. You deposit in Wise Vaults, you don't lock a position.

Where the 40% comes from

Every SOVR settled on the network splits by hard rule: 50% burned and recycled, 25% to the operator pool, 25% to the company. The operator pool is real revenue from real usage — and it funds your rate.

Your deposit becomes machines

USDC deposits purchase node licenses and hardware. The nodes serve real services, get paid in SOVR under published rules, and the pool's revenue pays you. Money in, machines working, revenue out.

Why Tier 01 is the one

Only 10,000 licenses will ever exist. Tier 01 is $3,000, the 40% APR is guaranteed for five months, and a large December deposit is expected to cap the pool. Later tiers are projected well under 40%.

Tier 01 — closing window
The clock

Why first movers win.

A very large deposit is scheduled to arrive in December. When it lands, the 40% APR node pool caps — closed to new deposits. After the cap, re-entry is secondary sell orders only, at whatever the market asks.

The platform team projects the next pool at ~60% APR, potentially three digits as the network grows — but node prices rise with each tier, so later entries buy in worse. First movers lock the rate, keep the full window, and enter at the lowest price.

Sources: platform team announcements (Oct 25 and 31, 2026) and the live dashboard. Projections are the team's, not ours — nothing is guaranteed, and rates change.

Questions everyone asks

Straight answers.

Is my deposit guaranteed?
Honest split: your deposit is USDC, a dollar stablecoin — your principal does not swing with token prices. That is the strongpoint: both rates pay on stable money. It is not risk-free: USDC is built to hold $1 but is issued by a company and has wobbled before; the yield depends on the platform reward buffer and real asset revenue; exits can have a queue; and the contracts, while exact, are still software. Stable describes your deposit, not the future. Do your own research, and never deposit money you need back on a schedule.
Do you hold my money?
No. Wise Stake Consulting never touches your funds. You deposit from your own wallet into the protocol. We earn referral commission from the platform on deposits made through our links — that's our disclosure, up front.
What happens after the 40% pool caps?
Existing positions keep earning their locked rate for the rest of the window. New entries close. The platform team has stated the next pool will open at a different rate with higher node prices — their projection is ~60% APR, potentially three digits as the network grows. Nothing is set in stone until the capital lands.
What's the actual difference between the 20% and the 40%?
Two different engines. The 20% pool is funded by World Mobile's telecom revenue and compounds automatically (APY). The 40% pool is funded by Sovren's node-economy revenue, is published as APR because it does not compound for you (compound it yourself and the effective return is higher), and carries the Tier 01 closing window. Both pay in stablecoin; both are deposited from the same app.
Can I get the phone plan without depositing, or deposit without the plan?
Either, or both. The phone plan is just a better bill. The vault is just a better yield. Run one, the other, or stack them — plenty of members pay for their plan out of the yield.
