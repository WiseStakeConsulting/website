The real difference

Every other network is owned by a corporation. This one is owned by its people.

Verizon's towers belong to Verizon. When you pay your bill, the revenue flows one way: up.

World Mobile flips it. The network is built out of AirNodes — telecom hardware owned by ordinary people. Buy one, the team deploys it where connectivity is needed, and when subscribers make calls, send texts, and use data through it, the owner earns. Real hardware, real usage, real earnings in USD, tracked live on a dashboard.

The result is a network with skin in the game: the people covering the planet with signal are the same people earning from it. Over 13,000 AirNodes are already live across the US, Africa, and Asia.

Straight answers

Questions we get first.

Is the 20% APY guaranteed?
It's published by the platform as a fixed yield on the World Mobile stablecoin strategy, funded by network revenue. Fixed is not the same as guaranteed — rates are set by the platform team and nothing is final until your deposit lands. We don't give financial advice; do your own research.
What exactly is an AirNode?
Telecom hardware on the World Mobile network. You buy it, the team deploys and activates it, and when subscribers use the network through it, you earn rewards in USD. EmberNodes start at $165 and Frontiers at $585, paid in full at checkout.
Do I need crypto experience to deposit?
You need a non-custodial wallet (MetaMask or Rabby) and USDC. Our deposit-ready guide walks through the wallet, the onramp, and the deposit step by step. Or book a free session and we'll do it together.
How do withdrawals work?
These are deposits, not locked stakes: a withdrawal is processed as a sell order — the platform sells the underlying position and pays you out. Per the platform team, sell orders are processed quickly, but it's an order queue, not an instant ATM. Plan your timeline accordingly.
Will World Mobile work in my area?
The fastest check is the ZIP-code coverage tool on the plans page. Activation is by eSIM in minutes, with no credit check and no contract.
Why does Wise Stake care about a phone company?
Because the thesis is the same one behind everything we do: the people using a system should own a piece of it and earn from it — whether that's a staked position, a piece of network hardware, or your own paycheck.
