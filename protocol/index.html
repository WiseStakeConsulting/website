PBS-1

Patronage Bundle Standard - binding creative works to transferable staking positions.

Specification v0.3 • under technical review • public release pending

"Art that funds itself. Positions that outlive the gallery. Patrons who get their capital back."

The PBS-1 idea, in three lines
Architecture

Three streams, one bundle

A PBS-1 bundle binds three things into a single NFT that the patron holds in their own wallet.

The Work

A creative work - art, music, writing, a collectible - escrowed in the bundle contract. It belongs to the bundle, and the bundle belongs to the patron.

The Position

A staking position (365 days to 42 years) held by the bundle. The position generates protocol distributions. The patron controls the scrape - and scraping has a cost in future accrual, so patience pays.

The Rail

A referral attribution pinned at creation, routing a share of protocol economics to the creator and distributor - forever, without either of them ever holding the patron's funds.

Design law

The non-negotiables

Every PBS-1 implementation, without exception:

1. Ownerless core. The bundle factory has no owner, no pause, no upgrade key. It can be killed by nothing.
2. Patron custody. The patron's wallet holds the bundle. No custodian, no intermediary, no exceptions - custody from block one.
3. Honest horizons. Terms are stated exactly: up to 42 years. No "forever." Distributions are described, never promised.
4. Zero-balance invariant. The factory holds nothing between transactions. It cannot be drained because there is nothing to drain.
5. Disclosed rails. Every party earning on a bundle's referral rail is named to the buyer in plain language, before purchase.
Custody

The purchase ceremony

Two flows exist. Flow A - the default - is fully non-custodial: the buyer funds the stake from their own wallet in a single transaction, the bundle is minted straight to them, and no third party ever holds their funds or the components. Flow B covers galleries pre-minting bundles for exhibition: legitimate, but custodial until sale, and it must be disclosed as such. The specification's buyer security checklist - exact approvals, verified splitter derivation, key-management warnings - is normative for every interface.
